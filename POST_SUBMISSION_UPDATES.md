## Update 1 — Composite Visual Prescriptions in the §H Herbal Section

**Date of first internal pre-registration:** 2026-06-05
**Date of public record:** 2026-06-09
**Author:** Frederick Davis Stalnecker, THEOS Research Institute

### The finding

The herbal-section (§H) illustrations of Beinecke MS 408 do not portray
single botanical species, as has been universally assumed across six
centuries of Voynich scholarship. They are **composite visual
prescriptions** — multi-ingredient medical formulations rendered as a
single fused plant, where the root, stem, leaves, flower, and fruit
each correspond to a DIFFERENT real-world botanical species. Together,
the component species encode the multi-ingredient recipe of a specific
medicinal preparation.

This reframing accounts for the long-standing observation that no §H
plant illustration maps cleanly to a single living botanical species:
the illustrations were never meant to. Each one is a recipe drawn as
a single composite plant.

### Two interlocking decode layers

The Voynich pharmacopeia is decoded through two parallel layers:

- **Textual layer (Cryptologia submission, §4–§6):** the 17-position
  Alberti volvelle at f57v, calibrated at corpus-baseline rotation
  R=14 with five section-specific rotations R ∈ {0, 7, 9, 11, 14},
  produces Middle Mongolian / Tibetan Sowa Rigpa phonemic content from
  the EVA-transcribed text. This layer yielded five CONFIRMED plant
  identifications in the submitted paper: *Punica granatum* (f2v),
  *Acorus calamus* (f6r), *Rheum* sp. (f3r), *Artemisia sieversiana*
  (f17v), and *Saussurea costus* (f24v), plus the Triphala anchor
  *Terminalia chebula* (f6v) confirmed under a separate pre-registration.

- **Visual layer (this post-submission update):** the §H illustrations
  themselves operate as composite visual prescriptions. Components
  within each illustration (root, leaves, flower, fruit) are identified
  using a comparative iconographic reference framework that matches
  Voynich morphological components against single-plant exemplars in
  contemporary medieval naturalistic herbal manuscripts.

The two layers are complementary: the textual layer identifies the
primary phonemic plant entry per folio; the visual layer identifies
the multi-ingredient component plants in the composite illustration of
the same folio.

### Validation — cross-folio component anchors

Independent THEOS multi-witness adversarial analysis was performed on
three §H folios (f33r, f52r, f51r). Four AI image-analysis systems from
DIFFERENT training families — used in adversarial configuration —
independently converged on identifying a common *Mandragora*-class
root morphology across all three folios. The training families
involved were OpenAI (GPT-4o), Google (Gemini 2.5), xAI (Grok 4.2),
and Perplexity (Sonar Pro). Convergence across non-overlapping
training families is taken as strong evidence that the visual signal
is real rather than an artifact of any single model's training data.

### Component-level identifications (preliminary)

The visual layer has produced multiple component identifications
across the §H herbal section. Strongest anchors:

- **f33r, f52r, f51r — root component** → *Mandragora* (cross-folio
  anchor confirmed by four independent AI training families)
- **f2v — leaf component** → *Asarum* (wild ginger / asarabacca)
- **f30r — flower component** → *Iacintus* (medieval *Hyacinthus* class)
- **f50r — flower component** → anthropomorphic / symbolic marker
  consistent with the face-in-flower convention documented in
  contemporary herbal traditions

Additional component identifications across the Phase 2 and Phase 3
pilot folios are recorded in the THEOS2 working archive and reserved
for the standalone publication noted below.

### Effect on the submitted Cryptologia paper

The submitted manuscript (ID 264889452) presents the textual decode
of the Voynich Manuscript as a Middle Mongolian rGyud bzhi
pharmaceutical encyclopedia, including the five CONFIRMED textual
plant identifications listed above. **The composite-prescription
finding does not alter, contradict, or supersede the submitted paper's
claims.** It adds a parallel interpretive layer at the illustration
level that complements the textual decode. The submitted paper's
textual identifications remain the phonemic anchors; the
composite-prescription work documents the multi-ingredient visual
recipes within which those textual anchors appear.
